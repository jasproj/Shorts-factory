# ElevenLabs voice recording script — Jay / Wander

Goal: ~30 minutes of clean audio for a Professional Voice Clone.
Part A (read) ≈ 15 min · Part B (freestyle prompts) ≈ 15 min.

## Before you hit record
- **Room:** smallest, softest room you have. A walk-in closet full of clothes is ideal. No fan, no AC, no TV.
- **Phone:** Voice Memos → Settings → Audio Quality → **Lossless**. Phone 6–8 inches from your mouth, slightly off to the side. Same spot, same distance every session.
- **Energy:** exactly how you want the Shorts to sound — casual, confident, a little punchy. Like answering a buddy's question at a bar. Not a radio voice, not a salesman.
- **Mistakes:** don't stop. Pause two seconds, say the sentence again. Clean takes matter more than perfect ones.
- **Split it up:** 5–10 minute files are fine. Do it all in one sitting if you can so your voice stays consistent.
- **Don't:** do accents, whisper, shout, or read like you're reading. If a line feels stiff, say it your way.

## PART A — Read these (≈15 min)

### A1. The Short answers (read each one twice: once normal, once a little faster)

Can you swim in the bio bay? In two of the three — no. Vieques and Fajardo are protected, kayak only. La Parguera, on the southwest coast, is the one where you can actually get in. Boat out, then you jump in.

Your Vieques tour didn't get cancelled for weather. It was the moon. Puerto Rico doesn't allow Mosquito Bay tours the day before, the day of, or the day after a full moon. Book your bio bay night around the moon first.

Brightest? Mosquito Bay on Vieques — it held the world record. But it's a ferry or a flight, and you're staying the night. Only have one night from San Juan? Laguna Grande in Fajardo, about an hour away.

On a full moon? Vieques won't even run. Fajardo will — but they sell it as a moonlight tour, not a glow tour. The darker the night, the brighter the bay. If you want the glow, go near the new moon.

Day trip to the Vieques bio bay? Not really. Tours run after dark, and getting there is a ferry from Ceiba or a flight. Plan to stay the night. If you can't, Fajardo is the move — about an hour from San Juan.

Is it worth it? Yes — if you go in with the right expectations. It's not a solid neon pool. It's a blue-green flash every time the water moves. Nobody can promise the glow, so pick a dark night and it pays off.

What does it actually look like? Every time your paddle hits the water, it flashes blue-green. Your hand, a fish swimming by — anything that moves the water lights it up. It's tiny living organisms, and they glow when disturbed.

Will your phone pick it up? Mostly, no. Bioluminescence is notoriously hard to photograph. Put the phone away and just watch. That's why every video you've seen, including this one, undersells it.

Why does it glow? Tiny organisms called dinoflagellates. They flash blue-green when something moves the water. And they're fed by the mangroves around the bay. Lose the mangroves, lose the glow.

What to bring? Here's the one people get wrong: skip the DEET bug spray in Mosquito Bay. It harms the organisms that make the glow. Yes, in a place called Mosquito Bay. You might itch for a day. Worth it.

Is it still glowing? Yes. After Hurricane Maria in 2017, Mosquito Bay went dark for about seven months. Then it came back. These bays are fragile — rain and storms affect them — but it's glowing again.

Can kids do it? Usually, yes — most Fajardo kayak tours start at age six, and they're built for beginners. Younger kids ride in the same kayak as an adult. Under six? Look at the La Parguera boat tours instead.

### A5. A longer stretch (read like you're telling a friend the story)

So here's how I'd do Puerto Rico's bio bays if it were my trip. First thing, before flights, before hotels — I check the moon. The glow is there year-round, but moonlight washes it out, and on Vieques they won't even run tours around the full moon. So I pick a night near the new moon and I build the trip around that.

Then it's one question: how many nights do I have? If it's one night and I'm in San Juan, I'm going to Fajardo. Laguna Grande. It's about an hour's drive, you check in at Las Croabas, and you paddle through this narrow mangrove channel in the dark. That channel is half the fun. It's tight, it's quiet, and by the time you come out into the lagoon your eyes have adjusted. Then every paddle stroke lights up.

If I've got a few days, I'm going to Vieques. You take the ferry from Ceiba or you fly, you stay on the island, and you go to Mosquito Bay. It's the brightest one, and it's the one people talk about for years. Skip the DEET. Leave the phone. Just watch your hand move through the water.

And if I want to actually get in the water, that's La Parguera, on the southwest side. It's a boat out, and you can swim. It's not as bright as Vieques, but you're in it, which is a whole different experience.

That's it. Moon first. Then pick your bay. Then go.

## PART B — Freestyle (≈15 min, talk ~1–2 min per prompt, no script)

Just talk. Ums are fine. This is the part that makes the clone sound like *you*.

1. Tell me about the best trip you've ever taken. What made it great?
2. Explain to a friend why they should never book a tour on the full moon.
3. Talk about Key West — what you'd tell someone going for the first time.
4. Describe kayaking at night to someone who's never done it.
5. What's a travel mistake you've made, and what would you do differently?
6. Rant (friendly) about tourist traps and how to avoid them.
7. Explain what you do for a living to someone at a party.
8. Read three comments out loud like you just found them, and react to each one.
9. Give your honest take: is it better to plan everything or wing it on vacation?
10. Talk about Tampa — what you'd show a visitor in one day.
11. Sell me on Puerto Rico in sixty seconds.
12. Wrap up like the end of a video: thank people, tell them to drop questions in the comments.
